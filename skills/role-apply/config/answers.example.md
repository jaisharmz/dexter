# Free-text seeds

Copy this file to `answers.md` in the same folder and replace every placeholder with your
own material. `answers.md` is yours and never committed: `config/` is gitignored apart
from the examples.

## Read this before writing any essay

**Do not invent the mechanism of a project.** The résumé gives the outcome, not the
method. Filling the method in with a plausible engineering narrative produces prose that
reads well and is false, in the exact place a technical interviewer will probe. If the
mechanism is not written down here, in the persona or on the résumé, leave it out or ask.

Application forms ask four or five questions in a hundred phrasings. Treat what follows
as raw material, not as answers. **Adapt every one to the specific role and firm.** The same
reader sees both applications when the operator applies to two roles at one company, and
two identical paragraphs read as a mail merge.

Every claim here should carry a URL or a number that a reader could check. Do not add one
that does not.

---

## "Anything else we should know?" / optional boxes

Usually leave these blank. An optional box filled with filler is worse than an empty one.
Fill it only when there is a specific, checkable fact the form had nowhere else to put: a
referral, a relevant repository, or the reason a date on the résumé looks odd. If you keep
a standing accomplishments list for these boxes, write it here in the form you want
pasted:

    > <role, company: what you built, with one concrete result>
    > <publications, patents or competitions, with counts or ranks>

---

## "How did you hear about us?"

Almost always a dropdown, usually required, and a factual question about the operator,
not a free choice. **Ask.** Common truthful answers are a university career fair, a
referral (name the person), the firm's own campus event, LinkedIn, or a recruiter who
reached out. Pick "Other" only when nothing fits, and never claim an event you did not
attend.
